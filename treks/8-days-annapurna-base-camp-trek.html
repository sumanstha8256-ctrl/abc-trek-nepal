Expedition Architecture

What Is the 8 Days Annapurna Base Camp Trek?

The 8-Day Annapurna Base Camp Trek is a Pokhara-to-Pokhara guided teahouse trek reaching Annapurna Base Camp at 4,130 m (13,550 ft), covering 65–85 km depending on the route variant. The trek carries a moderate difficulty rating, requires a licensed mountain guide and the Annapurna Conservation Area Permit (ACAP), and runs best during spring (March–May) and autumn (September–November).

This “8 days” count starts and ends in Pokhara; Kathmandu–Pokhara transfer days sit outside it and get added separately by most agencies. That distinction matters because it is not consistent across the market. At least one currently-ranking “8-Day ABC Trek” package actually describes nine days of on-the-ground activity—arrival, seven trekking days, and a separate Kathmandu return leg—under an “8 Days” title, and its own itinerary text refers to itself as a “nine-day itinerary” in places. A trekker comparing quotes should confirm whether a stated day count is Pokhara-to-Pokhara only, or includes Kathmandu transfers, before treating two “8-day” prices as comparable.

What Annapurna Base Camp Actually Is

The trek’s destination sits inside the Annapurna Sanctuary, a glacial basin enclosed on all sides by peaks above 6,000 m. British mountaineer Jimmy Roberts entered the basin in 1956 and named it “Sanctuary” for the wall-like ring of summits surrounding it. Annapurna I, the massif’s highest point at 8,091 m, holds a legendary place in mountaineering history: French climbers Maurice Herzog and Louis Lachenal reached its summit on 3 June 1950, making it the first peak above 8,000 m ever climbed by any expedition—predating the first ascent of Everest by three years. Meanwhile, Machhapuchhre (6,993 m), the sharp “Fishtail” peak visible throughout the trek, remains strictly unclimbed by official government policy; Nepal has never issued a summit permit for it, since the mountain holds profound sacred significance to local Gurung communities.

The Protected Conservation Area

The entire route lies inside the Annapurna Conservation Area (ACA), a 7,629 km² protected zone established in 1986—Nepal’s first and largest conservation area, managed by the National Trust for Nature Conservation (NTNC). The ACA is home to over 100 mammal species, including the endangered red panda and elusive snow leopard, and more than 470 bird species, among them the Himalayan monal (Danphe), Nepal’s national bird. All ACAP permit fees fund the area’s forest conservation, trail maintenance, and community health programs directly.

Field Schedule

Day-by-Day 8-Day Expedition Itinerary

Paced for optimal acclimatization, scenic diversity, and cultural immersion between Nayapul and Jhinu Danda.

The 8-day Poon Hill itinerary covers 6 full trekking days between Nayapul and Jhinu Danda, with the largest single-day elevation gain occurring on Day 6 (+900 m) during the final push from Deurali to Annapurna Base Camp via Machhapuchhre Base Camp. Daily walking time ranges from 4 to 8 hours, with the descent from Annapurna Base Camp on Day 7 covering the greatest single-day distance (15 km).

Morning departure from Pokhara Lakeside by private vehicle to the Nayapul trailhead (1,070 m). Clear ACAP permits and guide paperwork at the Birethanti checkpoint (1,025 m), where the Modi Khola and Bhurungdi Khola rivers converge. Follow the dirt track along the river through sub-tropical farmland to Tikhedhunga. From Tikhedhunga, begin the infamous stone staircase climb—over 3,200 continuous stone steps carved into the hillside—gaining 500 vertical meters up to the scenic Magar village of Ulleri (2,070 m).

Lead Guide Field Note · Sugam Shrestha

The Tikhedhunga-to-Ulleri stone staircase is the steepest sustained leg of the entire trek. Do not rush. Maintain a rhythmic, short-stepping cadence (“bistari, bistari”) and keep trekking poles adjusted slightly shorter on the steep steps to preserve quadriceps power for the days ahead.

Climb steadily through cool, moss-draped forests of oak, hemlock, and giant rhododendron trees. Cross tumbling crystalline streams at Banthanti (2,210 m) and Nangethanti (2,430 m), a popular lunch stop. As you ascend toward the ridge, the air cools noticeably. Arrive at upper Ghorepani (2,860 m), an ancient horse-caravan staging post on the trade route between Pokhara and Mustang. The village offers immediate westward panoramas of Dhaulagiri I (8,167 m) and Annapurna South.

Lead Guide Field Note · Sugam Shrestha

In spring (March–April), this entire forest corridor explodes in crimson, scarlet, and pink blooms. Prepare warm layers and headlamps tonight; tomorrow morning begins with an early 4:30 AM wake-up call for the pre-dawn hike up Poon Hill.

Pre-dawn 45-minute headlamp climb up stone steps to the summit viewpoint of Poon Hill (3,210 m). Witness the 360-degree Himalayan sunrise sweeping across Dhaulagiri (8,167 m), Annapurna I (8,091 m), Machhapuchhre (6,993 m), and Nilgiri. Return to the lodge for hot breakfast, pack bags, and begin trekking east toward the Modi Khola canyon. Climb along Deurali ridge (3,100 m) with razor-edge views before descending through a deeply carved forest gorge with waterfalls to the ridge settlement of Tadapani (2,630 m).

Lead Guide Field Note · Sugam Shrestha

Poon Hill provides the only vantage on this trek where you see both Dhaulagiri (the world’s 7th highest) and Annapurna I (the 10th highest) side-by-side. Once at Annapurna Base Camp itself, Dhaulagiri sits behind the sanctuary wall and is no longer visible.

Descend through dense rhododendron and bamboo groves to Kimrong Khola, cross the suspension bridge, and climb up the terraced agricultural amphitheater of Chhomrong (2,170 m)—the gateway to the Sanctuary and the highest permanent Gurung settlement. After lunch in Chhomrong, descend 2,500 stone steps down to the Chhomrong Khola bridge, then ascend steeply through Upper Sinuwa (2,360 m). From Sinuwa, enter the narrow Modi Khola gorge, entering dense bamboo forest to sleep at Bamboo (2,310 m).

Lead Guide Field Note · Sugam Shrestha

Beyond Chhomrong, strict Annapurna Sanctuary cultural rules take effect: no live poultry or pork products can be carried into the sacred valley, and single-use plastic mineral water bottles are banned. We refill from certified safe ozonated water stations.

Trek through humid bamboo thickets to Dovan (2,600 m), passing small forest shrines honoring the forest deities Baraha and Pancha Kanya. Ascend above the tree line past the historic rock overhang of Hinku Cave (3,120 m)—used as shelter by early mountaineering expeditions. The canyon narrows sharply between the sheer rock cliffs of Hiunchuli and Machhapuchhre. Cross avalanche-chute boulder beds to reach Deurali (3,230 m), located in a dramatic canyon throat.

Lead Guide Field Note · Sugam Shrestha

You cross the 3,000m threshold today. Temperatures drop substantially once the sun dips behind the canyon walls at 3:00 PM. Our team conducts baseline pulse oximetry (SpO2) checks this evening at Deurali.

Follow the trail along the lateral moraine of the Modi Khola as the canyon dramatically broadens into an expansive alpine valley. Climb steadily to Machhapuchhre Base Camp (MBC, 3,700 m), nestled directly below the colossal sheer face of the Fishtail peak. After tea at MBC, enter the true Annapurna Sanctuary basin. A gentle, steady 2-hour incline through moraine meadows leads directly into Annapurna Base Camp (4,130 m). You are surrounded by a complete 360-degree wall of giants: Annapurna I (8,091 m), Annapurna South (7,219 m), Hiunchuli (6,441 m), Machhapuchhre (6,993 m), and Gangapurna (7,455 m).

Lead Guide Field Note · Sugam Shrestha

Day 6 gains 900 m from Deurali to ABC—the trip’s crucial acclimatization pivot point. Walk with deliberate pacing. Upon arrival, we settle into the lodge, rest with hot ginger lemon tea, and step out onto the rim for sunset as alpine glow lights up the south face of Annapurna I.

Early morning wake-up call for the golden sunrise across Annapurna I, Machhapuchhre, and the South Glacier. After breakfast, begin the long descent back down the Modi Khola valley. Because your body has acclimatized and gravity works in your favor, pace moves quickly down through MBC, Deurali, and Himalaya. Re-enter the sheltered bamboo forest canopy to spend the night at Bamboo (2,310 m).

Lead Guide Field Note · Sugam Shrestha

Day 7 covers the single greatest walking distance of the entire trek (15 km) and drops 1,820 m in elevation. Lock your trekking poles slightly longer for downhill stability to absorb knee shock.

Climb up from Bamboo through Sinuwa and conquer the stone steps up to Chhomrong village for morning tea. Descend the southern ridge down to Jhinu Danda (1,780 m). Take a rejuvenating 20-minute walk down to the natural riverside thermal pools along the Modi Khola to soothe trekking muscles. Cross the dramatic 287-meter Jhinu suspension bridge to the Siwai roadhead, where your private 4WD jeep awaits to drive you back to your hotel in Pokhara Lakeside (822 m).

Lead Guide Field Note · Sugam Shrestha

Celebration dinner in Pokhara Lakeside tonight! You have completed over 80 kilometers through the Annapurna Sanctuary, stood beneath six 6,000m+ peaks, and experienced the finest teahouse trails in the Himalayas.

Six named peaks above 6,000 m, two distinct base camps, riverside thermal springs, and centuries-old Gurung stone settlements.

Trail Physics

What Distance and Elevation Gain Does the Trek Cover?

The Via Poon Hill route covers approximately 80–85 km round-trip with roughly 3,300 m of net cumulative elevation gain, climbing from Nayapul (1,070 m) through Poon Hill (3,210 m) to Annapurna Base Camp (4,130 m). This is meaningfully longer than the Ghandruk-direct (65–70 km) or Jhinu Danda drive-in (55–65 km) variants, precisely because it adds the Ghorepani–Poon Hill loop.

Why published distance figures disagree so sharply across sources:
  • Route variant spread: The 20 km difference between the Poon Hill loop and the direct drive-in variants accounts for most of the variation online.
  • Trailhead location: Nayapul versus Siwai or Samrung drive-in alters starting distance by 15–20 kilometers.
  • Internal checking: Several competing ranking pages state a “30 km maximum one-way” figure that contradicts their own day-by-day itineraries (which sum closer to 40–42 km one-way). The numbers in our Section 3 itinerary table sum to approximately 85 km, completely verified on the ground.
Physical Preparation

How Difficult Is the 8-Day Itinerary?

Rated Moderate trending Strenuous due to Day 1’s stone steps and Day 7’s long downhill descent.

The 8 Days Annapurna Base Camp Trek carries a moderate difficulty rating, requiring 4 to 8 hours of daily walking on stone staircases, forest trails, and alpine moraine terrain, with zero technical mountaineering skills required. It is, however, more physically demanding than 10- or 12-day itineraries because the same distance is packed into fewer days.

Day 1 Stone Staircases

+1,000m gain over 3,200 uneven stone steps to Ulleri before your body has acclimatized.

Day 7 Descent Length

15 km and 1,820m of continuous descent in a single day, which punishes unprepared quadriceps.

Compressed Daily Margin

With only 8 days, there is less daily margin for an off day compared to 10–12 day routes.

Field Readiness Benchmarks

A trekker well-prepared for this itinerary meets these three physical baselines before arriving in Nepal:

  • Walks 8–10 km with 400–500 m of elevation gain without requiring extended next-day recovery.
  • Climbs multiple flights of stairs carrying an 8–10 kg daypack without stopping or gasping for breath.
  • Descends stairs or steep inclines for 30 continuous minutes without knee pain.

Structured 8-Week Pre-Expedition Training Schedule

Weeks 8–6 3–4 Sessions / Wk
Aerobic Base Building

Brisk hill walking, outdoor hiking, cycling, or stair-master sessions to build foundational aerobic lung capacity.

Weeks 6–4 2–3 Sessions / Wk
Stair & Incline Power

Add loaded stair climbing and eccentric quad exercises (lunges, step-downs) to prepare knees for stone steps.

Weeks 4–2 2 Sessions / Wk
Loaded Pack Practice

Hike 10–14 km on undulating terrain carrying full daypack weight (8–10 kg) wearing your broken-in trekking boots.

Final 2 Weeks Light Activity
Taper & Recovery

Taper training volume, focus on mobility and deep sleep. Arrive in Kathmandu rested and free of muscle fatigue.

Training Tip: Downhill-specific training is what most trekkers skip, yet Day 7’s 15 km, 1,820 m descent punishes hardest. A few sessions of loaded stair descent will save your knees.

Trail Operations

What Do the Guide and Porter Actually Do?

The Guide’s Responsibilities

Navigation, pace setting, checkpoint clearances at Birethanti and Chhomrong, lodge room allocation, and daily briefings. On this compressed 8-day schedule, the guide’s most critical function is real-time health assessment: conducting daily pulse oximeter checks, monitoring early AMS symptoms, and making clinical judgment calls on descent.

The Porter’s Welfare & Role

Porters carry main duffels (10–12 kg per trekker), enabling you to walk unburdened with only daypack essentials (water, camera, layers). Under strict IPPG ethical guidelines, ABC Trek in Nepal caps loads at 20 kg per porter, provides warm altitude apparel, footwear, and full medical/rescue insurance for all crew members.

Teahouse Hospitality

What Is Accommodation and Food Like on This Route?

Accommodation consists of family-run mountain lodges known as teahouses. Room facilities naturally simplify as you gain altitude:

Lower Elevations (Ulleri, Ghorepani, Chhomrong): Twin-share wooden rooms, attached bathrooms frequently available, reliable hot showers, and Wi-Fi.
Mid-Elevation (Tadapani, Bamboo): Twin-share wooden rooms with shared bathroom facilities. Hot bucket showers available for a small fee.
High Alpine (Deurali, ABC): Basic stone rooms with twin beds, foam mattresses, pillows, and blankets. Shared or squat toilets. Hot water and charging are weather-dependent and limited.

Trail Food & Dal Bhat: Dal Bhat (steamed rice, spiced lentil soup, vegetable curry, and spinach, served with unlimited complimentary refills) is the king of trekking meals—“Dal Bhat Power 24 Hour.” Menus also offer porridge, pancakes, Tibetan bread, vegetable noodles, fried rice, and garlic soup. Vegetarian food is fresh and widely available; dairy-free vegan diets are readily accommodated.

Medical Safety

How Does Altitude Affect an 8-Day Compressed Schedule?

Annapurna Base Camp sits at 4,130 m, well above the 2,500 m threshold where Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS) can occur.

Because this itinerary moves from Deurali (3,230 m) directly to Annapurna Base Camp (4,130 m) on Day 6 with zero rest days built in, understanding acclimatization is essential. AMS risk correlates with ascent speed, not gym fitness.

Our Altitude Clinical Protocols
  • Twice-Daily Pulse Oximeter Checks: Your lead guide logs blood oxygen saturation (SpO2) and resting pulse every morning and evening above 2,500m.
  • Hydration Benchmark: Drink 3.5 to 4 liters of purified water daily to aid respiratory acclimatization.
  • The Cardinal Rule: Never ascend with symptoms of AMS (throbbing headache, nausea, dizziness). If symptoms persist or worsen, immediate descent is the only definitive treatment.

What Should Trekkers Pack for This Trek?

Temperatures range from +22°C in Pokhara to −5°C overnight at ABC. A modular 4-layer system is essential.

1. Layering Apparel
  • Thermal base layers (merino wool or synthetic)
  • Fleece mid-layer jacket
  • Waterproof & windproof shell jacket (Gore-Tex)
  • Down jacket rated to −10°C (can rent in Pokhara)
2. Footwear & Trail Gear
  • Broken-in waterproof trekking boots
  • 3–4 pairs merino trekking socks
  • Adjustable trekking poles (mandatory for Day 1 & 7)
  • Camp shoes / sandals for teahouses
3. Sleeping & Headwear
  • Four-season sleeping bag (rated to 0°C to −5°C)
  • Warm beanie & UV sun hat
  • Headlamp with spare batteries (Poon Hill pre-dawn)
  • UV-blocking sunglasses & SPF 50 sunscreen
4. Medical & Personal
