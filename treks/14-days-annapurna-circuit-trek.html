Expedition Architecture

What Is the 14-Day Annapurna Circuit Trek?

The 14-Day Annapurna Circuit Trek is a guided Himalayan trekking package led by Sugam Shrestha that circles the Annapurna Massif in Nepal, starting and ending in Kathmandu. The trek crosses Thorong La Pass at 5,416 meters, the route's highest point, and passes through the Annapurna Conservation Area across four primary districts: Lamjung, Manang, Mustang, and Myagdi, before finishing in Kaski district at Pokhara.

Trekkers walk between 4 and 8 hours a day on 9 active hiking days out of the 14-day itinerary, separated by vehicle transit days and one essential acclimatization rest day in Manang, gaining over 4,500 meters of cumulative elevation climb between the lowlands and the pass. The route follows the Marsyangdi River gorge on its eastern ascent and the sacred Kali Gandaki River on its western descent, connecting subtropical lemon groves to high-altitude Tibetan desert terrain within a single journey.

The Annapurna Conservation Area Project (ACAP) manages the region the trek passes through. Covering 7,629 square kilometers, ACAP is Nepal's premier protected area. The trek's defining feature is the true loop architecture: trekkers ascend one valley and descend an entirely different valley on the other side, rather than retracing the same trail—which distinguishes the Annapurna Circuit from out-and-back treks like the Annapurna Base Camp Sanctuary.

Stage 2: Days 5–7

Stage 4: Days 11–12

Ghorepani → Sunrise Poon Hill (3,210m) → Nayapul → Pokhara Lakeside → Return Kathmandu. (Compare with Ghorepani Poon Hill Trek).

Visual Highlights

6 Signature Moments on the 14-Day Circuit

From the highest navigable mountain pass on earth to sacred Hindu-Buddhist pilgrimage shrines and golden dawn viewpoints.

Day 10 · 5,416m Peak

1. Thorong La Pass Crossing (5,416m)

Standing at the pass crest draped in thousands of wind-whipped Buddhist prayer flags, looking across the vast Annapurna Massif to the east and the dry Tibetan plateau of Mustang to the west. Managed under clinical altitude safety protocols to ensure safe dawn passage.

Days 6–7 · 3,540m Basin

2. Manang Enclave & Glacial Amphitheater

Ancient flat-roofed stone settlements beneath the sheer vertical ice wall of Gangapurna. Day 7 includes active acclimatization walks to Gangapurna glacial lake, Ice Lake, or cliffside gompas, with the option to attend daily high-altitude lectures at the Himalayan Rescue Association (HRA) Manang Clinic.

Day 10 · 3,760m Shrine

3. Sacred Muktinath Temple & 108 Spouts

Revered by both Hindus and Buddhists as Chumig Gyatsa ("Hundred Waters"), this sacred complex features 108 brass gargoyle water spouts, an eternal natural gas flame, and holy Vishnu shrines. (Explore our dedicated Jomsom Muktinath Trek).

Day 13 · 3,210m Viewpoint

4. Golden Dawn Sunrise from Poon Hill

A pre-dawn hike to Poon Hill's viewing tower rewards you with a golden sunrise illuminating Dhaulagiri (8,167m), Annapurna I (8,091m), Annapurna South, and the holy Fishtail peak above a sea of valley fog. Also available as a standalone short walk: Ghorepani Poon Hill Sunrise Trek.

5. Kali Gandaki Gorge & Marpha Apples

Walk the world's deepest river canyon between 8,000-meter giants and taste sweet freshly baked apple pies and apple brandy in the whitewashed Thakali stone village of Marpha.

6. Tatopani Geothermal Hot Springs

Soothe your legs after descending 4,200 vertical meters from Thorong La in warm, natural mineral hot spring pools positioned right beside the rushing waters of the Kali Gandaki River.

Field Schedule

Complete 14-Day Expedition Itinerary

Paced for optimal safety, terrain enjoyment, and cultural immersion. Click any day to view trail telemetry, route notes, and guide advice.

9 Trekking Days · 4 Drive Days · 1 Rest Day

Following your private airport transfer from Tribhuvan International Airport (KTM), depart Kathmandu early in the morning via private 4WD vehicle along the Prithvi Highway, tracing the turbulent Trishuli River before following the Marsyangdi River into Lamjung district. We pass through rural farming bazaars and reach the trailhead at Bhulbhule (840m).

Lead Guide Field Note

Sugam's Tip: Keep your camera ready as we cross into Lamjung; on clear afternoons, Lamjung Himal and the Manaslu range provide our first towering snow-peak vistas.

Today marks our first day on foot. The trail ascends gently through terraced rice fields, crossing suspension bridges over the thundering Marsyangdi. We pass through Gurung settlements, lush subtropical bamboo forests, and dramatic cliffside paths, reaching the historic toll station village of Jagat (1,300m).

Lead Guide Field Note

Stay well hydrated today as the lower gorge can be warm and humid. Apply sun protection early.

Climbing out of Jagat, we enter the district of Manang at Tal (1,700m), an extraordinary village situated on an ancient flat lakebed bordered by a huge cascading waterfall. The canyon narrows dramatically as we walk under sheer granite overhangs into Dharapani (1,860m), where the trail intersects the Manaslu Circuit.

We transition into temperate pine and fir forests, breathing in the crisp resin-scented air. Views of Annapurna II (7,937m) and Lamjung Himal begin to dominate the western skyline. We arrive in Chame (2,610m), the administrative headquarters of Manang district, featuring banks, gear shops, and therapeutic hot springs.

Crossing to the northern bank of the Marsyangdi, the valley opens to reveal the colossal Paungda Danda—a seamless curved rock slab rising 1,500m out of the riverbed, known locally as the Gateway to Heaven. We ascend to Upper Pisang (3,200m), home to an ancient Tibetan monastery with panoramic views of the Annapurna range.

We take the upper route via Ghyaru and Ngawal, offering some of the most spectacular balcony panoramas in the Himalayas. Gazing across the valley at Annapurna II, III, IV, and Gangapurna, the trail winds past ancient Buddhist chortens down into the broad, dry U-shaped Manang valley (3,540m).

A mandatory physiological acclimatization day. We practice the essential "climb high, sleep low" rule with an active morning hike up to Gangapurna Lake and viewpoint (3,800m) or the historic cliffside monastery of Chongkor. In the afternoon, we attend the daily high-altitude medical lecture conducted by the Himalayan Rescue Association (HRA) doctors in Manang.

Altitude Acclimatization Checklist

Drink at least 4 liters of boiled water today. We perform our evening pulse-oximeter check; any lingering headache or fatigue should be communicated to your guide immediately.

Leaving the main valley, we climb steadily above the tree line into high alpine tundra along the Jarsang Khola. We pass grazing yaks and herds of Himalayan blue sheep (bharal) browsing on thorny juniper scrub, reaching the cluster of lodges at Yak Kharka (4,050m).

A short, deliberate trekking day to conserve energy before the pass. We cross a suspension bridge and traverse a scree slope before reaching Thorong Phedi (4,525m) at the base of the pass. We conduct an early dinner, double-check headlamps, thermal layers, and crampons/microspikes, and retire by 7:30 PM.

The crowning achievement of the expedition. We begin our ascent at 3:30 AM under a dazzling canopy of Himalayan stars. Ascending a zigzagging mountain path in freezing temperatures (-10°C to -15°C), we reach High Camp (4,850m) as the horizon turns crimson.

After 4 to 5 hours of sustained uphill effort, we stand triumphantly at Thorong La Pass (5,416m), surrounded by prayer flags and 360-degree views of the Annapurna and Dhaulagiri massifs. After celebratory photographs and hot tea, we begin an 800m descent into the arid Tibetan desert of Mustang, reaching the sacred sanctuary of Muktinath (3,760m).

Summit Safety Briefing

Use trekking poles for the descent; trekking poles reduce knee impact by up to 25% over the 1,600m downhill drop to Muktinath.

After an early morning visit to Muktinath Temple, we take 4WD transport down through the windy Kali Gandaki valley via Jomsom and the apple orchards of Marpha. We continue past Ghasa and descend down to Tatopani (1,190m), where you can spend the afternoon soaking tired muscles in natural geothermal hot springs.

Leaving the Kali Gandaki, we ascend steadily through lush Magar villages like Sikha and Chitre into the world's largest rhododendron forest. We reach the ridge village of Ghorepani (2,860m), commanding panoramic views of Dhaulagiri I (8,167m) and the Annapurna chain.

Pre-dawn 45-minute climb to Poon Hill (3,210m) for an iconic Himalayan sunrise across Dhaulagiri, Annapurna I, Annapurna South, Nilgiri, and Machhapuchhre. Descend to Ghorepani for breakfast, then trek through Tikhedhunga and Ulleri to Nayapul, where our private vehicle drives us to our hotel in Pokhara Lakeside (820m).

Enjoy a relaxed breakfast beside Phewa Lake before our comfortable tourist coach or private vehicle journey back to Kathmandu along the scenic Prithvi Highway. Arrive in Thamel in the late afternoon for celebratory farewell dinner and shopping.

Medical Rigor

Altitude Sickness Protocols & Evacuation

How our medical guidelines ensure safe passage over Thorong La Pass, adhering to clinical standards from the Himalayan Rescue Association (HRA) and Wilderness Medical Society.

Condition Altitude Threshold Key Warning Signs Mandatory Emergency Action
AMS (Mild Mountain Sickness) Above 2,800m Persistent headache, nausea, mild dizziness, insomnia, fatigue Halt ascent immediately, rest day at current altitude, hydrate (4L), Diamox (125–250mg BID). Do not ascend.
HACE (Cerebral Edema) Above 3,500m Ataxia (heel-to-toe balance failure), confusion, irrational behavior, extreme lethargy Immediate mandatory descent (500–1,000m), oxygen, Dexamethasone 8mg, satellite helicopter evacuation.
HAPE (Pulmonary Edema) Above 3,000m Breathlessness at rest, persistent rattling cough, blue lips (cyanosis), frothy sputum Immediate mandatory descent (1,000m+), high-flow oxygen, Nifedipine, emergency helicopter evacuation.

Expert Answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Clear answers to the most common questions asked by prospective trekkers.

Thorong La Pass (5,416m) is the most demanding day of the itinerary. Trekkers begin at 3:30 AM with headlamps, ascending 891 meters over 4 to 5 hours in sub-zero temperatures (-10°C to -15°C) to the pass crest, followed by a steep 1,650-meter descent to Muktinath. While the trail is non-technical (no ice axes or ropes required), sustained leg strength, trekking poles, warm windproof clothing, and mental resilience are essential.

Yes, fit beginners can complete the trek. The route does not require mountaineering experience, but you must possess cardiovascular stamina built from 6 to 8 weeks of pre-trip conditioning (hill walking, jogging, stair stepping with a backpack). Our built-in rest day in Manang (3,540m) and gradual altitude progression ensure proper acclimatization.

Every foreign trekker requires the Annapurna Conservation Area Permit (ACAP) and the Trekkers' Information Management System (TIMS) card. Both permits, government fees, and checkpoint processing are 100% arranged and included in our package. Under Nepal Tourism Board rules, unguided solo trekking is prohibited.

Yes, comprehensive emergency medical insurance covering emergency helicopter evacuation up to 6,000 meters is strictly mandatory. We verify your insurance policy details before departing Kathmandu. If an evacuation is medically required, our operations team coordinates satellite helicopter scramble directly with your insurer.

Teahouse dining halls provide hearty, freshly prepared meals. The gold standard is traditional Nepali Dal Bhat (lentil soup, rice, and vegetable curry with free second helpings). Menus also offer porridge, Tibetan bread, eggs, fried noodles, momo dumplings, potato stews, and herbal teas. We recommend vegetarian meals above 3,000m for stomach safety.

The 14-day classic itinerary preserves the vital acclimatization rest day in Manang (3,540m), substantially reducing the risk of altitude sickness at Thorong La. In addition, it incorporates the soothing mineral hot springs of Tatopani and the world-famous sunrise from Poon Hill (3,210m), giving you the complete Himalayan experience rather than rushing by jeep.
