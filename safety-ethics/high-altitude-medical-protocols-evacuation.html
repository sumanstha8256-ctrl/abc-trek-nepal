High-Altitude Safety Protocols & Porter Welfare

High-altitude Himalayan trekking carries real physiological risks. At ABC Trek in Nepal, we eliminate guesswork through twice-daily pulse oximetry, strict Lake Louise AMS scoring, certified Wilderness First Responders, and uncompromising ethical treatment for our porter teams.

Medical Rigor

1. The Twice-Daily Pulse Oximetry Standard

Every lead mountain guide carries an FDA-certified fingertip pulse oximeter.

At breakfast and dinner above 2,500 meters, your guide measures and logs your blood oxygen saturation level (SpO2) and resting heart rate in your personal altitude record. This data establishes your body's acclimatization trendline and allows us to catch Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS) before clinical symptoms escalate.

Normal Range (2,500m – 3,500m)
SpO2: 85% – 93%

Typical expected physiological adaptation to decreased atmospheric pressure.

Caution Zone (3,500m – 4,130m)
SpO2: 75% – 84%

Requires monitoring, increased hydration, garlic soup, and rest.

Clinical Assessment

2. The Lake Louise AMS Scoring System

We do not rely on subjective complaints alone. Our guides utilize the international Lake Louise Acute Mountain Sickness scoring system evaluating 4 key criteria:

  • Headache: Mild (1 pt) to Severe/Incapacitating (3 pts)
  • Gastrointestinal Symptoms: Poor appetite (1 pt), nausea (2 pts), vomiting (3 pts)
  • Fatigue / Weakness: Mild fatigue (1 pt) to severe exhaustion (3 pts)
  • Dizziness / Lightheadedness: Mild (1 pt) to incapacitating (3 pts)
Descent Mandate

Any trekker exhibiting an AMS score of 5+ or signs of ataxia (loss of physical coordination) is immediately accompanied down the mountain to lower elevations (Bamboo or Chhomrong). We never leave an unwell trekker unattended.

Emergency Response

3. Helicopter Extraction & SOS Coordination

In the rare event of severe medical complications (such as High Altitude Pulmonary Edema / HAPE or severe trauma), our operations headquarters in Pokhara maintains direct standby communication with helicopter charter operators.

Designated helicopter landing coordinates are mapped at:

  • Annapurna Base Camp Helipad: 4,130m (Glacial moraine clearing)
  • Machhapuchhre Base Camp Helipad: 3,700m
  • Deurali Helipad: 3,230m
  • Chhomrong Helipad: 2,170m (Primary extraction zone in cloudy weather)
Human Dignity & Labor Rights

4. Our Porter Welfare Charter (IPPG Standard)

Porters are the backbone of the Himalayas. We adhere strictly to the International Porter Protection Group (IPPG) five core guidelines:

Weight Cap Guarantee
20kg Max Load

1 porter carries for max 2 trekkers (10kg each). We never overload our crew.

Cold-Weather Gear
Full Mountain Kit

Warm windproof jacket, sturdy footwear, gloves, and sunglasses provided.

Insurance Coverage
Full Medical & Rescue

100% insured for emergency hospital care and helicopter evacuation.

Fair Living Wages
Above TAAN Rates

Wages well above national standards, with full room and board provided.
